Modal interchange gets explained as a trick — "borrow a chord from the parallel minor" — as if it's a spice you sprinkle on an otherwise ordinary progression. That framing makes it sound decorative. On bass, where you're often the only instrument stating the root, it isn't decorative at all. It's a change of gravity.

Play a I–IV–V in C major and the bass line has a predictable center of mass — everything resolves back toward C, and your ear knows it before the chord even lands. Swap that IV for a iv (F minor instead of F major) and nothing about the rhythm or the harmony's basic shape changes, but the pull toward home suddenly has a shadow on it. The IV wasn't just a chord, it was a promise about what key you're in, and borrowing from the parallel minor breaks that promise for one bar without breaking the song.

> The interesting part isn't the borrowed chord itself. It's the half-step or whole-step the bass has to travel to get there, and back.

## Where it actually lives

Two spots do most of the work in the songs I actually play:

- **bVII** borrowed into a major key — the classic "one chord that makes a pop progression feel like it's leaning backward instead of forward"
- **iv** borrowed into a major key — same trick, softer, more melancholy, because it's minor where you expected major rather than a whole different chord altogether

Neither requires reharmonizing anything else. That's the part that took me longest to internalize — modal interchange isn't a modulation, you don't have to resolve it or justify it to the rest of the progression. It's a single chord allowed to disagree with the key signature for one bar, and then the song moves on like nothing happened.

## Why the bass notices first

Piano and guitar players can voice a borrowed chord ambiguously — leave out the third, let the listener decide what they heard. The bass can't. If you're playing the root, you've committed. That's why borrowed chords tend to feel more decisive from underneath than from on top — there's no hedge available to the instrument stating gravity.
